Financial Tracker

Privacy Policy

Effective and last updated: 11 November 2026

This policy explains how Financial Tracker (Android package com.ahmed.finflow), previously named Financial Advisor, and the companion web app at financialadvisorapp.pages.dev handle information. References to “we” or “the app” mean the developer and operation of these services.

Your information, at a glance
  • Financial records are provided by you. The app does not connect directly to your bank or initiate payments.
  • The Android app can keep records locally. Signing in with Google enables cloud features; the companion web app requires sign-in.
  • Optional Android SMS access lets the app detect financial messages, prepare transactions for your review and, only if you turn on automatic recording, save familiar ones for you.
  • Mentor works out its guidance on your phone. On supported phones, Gemini Nano, or an optional offline model you choose to download, words its answers on the device. Google’s ML Kit sends Google diagnostic and usage information, not your financial records.
  • We do not sell your personal information or use it for targeted advertising.

1. Information the app handles

Depending on the features you use, information may include:

  • Account information: your Google account email, authentication identity and session credentials used to sign in and keep your app records associated with your account. Google and our authentication provider may also supply basic profile information, such as your name or profile image.
  • Financial records: account names and types, currencies, balances, transaction amounts and dates, categories, subcategories, merchants, notes, labels, recurring plans, budgets, savings goals, debts, loans and project records.
  • Documents you choose: receipt images, warranty details, photos and project documents, including information visible in those documents.
  • Preferences and learned rules: language, appearance, exchange rates, monthly cycle settings, templates, reminders and financial-message classification rules you choose to remember.
  • Mentor information: the strategy, notes and preferences you give Mentor, questions you ask it and the replies it gives. See section 5.
  • Technical information: our hosting and authentication providers may process IP addresses, request times, browser/device information and security or error logs necessary to deliver and protect their services. On Android, Google’s ML Kit software also sends Google the diagnostic and usage information described in section 5.
  • Support correspondence: information you send when asking a question or requesting data deletion.

Do not enter bank passwords, card security codes, authentication codes or other credentials in transaction notes or uploaded documents. We do not need them.

2. How information is used

Information is used to display and organize your records, calculate summaries, budgets and balances, prepare drafts, remember your chosen classifications, save familiar SMS transactions when you turn on automatic recording, suggest categories, produce Mentor guidance, provide reminders, notifications and widgets, authenticate you, synchronize records when enabled, process backups and imports, respond to requests, and maintain security and reliability. We do not initiate bank transfers, purchase financial products or make lending decisions from your records.

3. Optional SMS access and message processing

The Android app requests RECEIVE_SMS and READ_SMS only when you choose to enable SMS features. These permissions can expose received SMS messages, including personal messages, sender details and timestamps. The app uses them as follows:

  • New messages: RECEIVE_SMS lets the app check each incoming message in the background. Messages that look financial are kept in an encrypted queue on your device and you are notified. Other messages, one-time passcodes and declined or failed payment messages are not kept; the app only records the time a message arrived.
  • Your inbox: READ_SMS lets the app display inbox messages, support its SMS widget and prepare transactions for review. It also checks today’s messages about every three hours, when you open Home or when you tap Check now, to find financial messages that were missed.

Messages are parsed on your device to suggest an amount, account, merchant, category or subcategory. Suggestions can be wrong; review them before saving.

Automatic recording is off by default and is set separately for each profile. If you turn it on, the app can save a transaction without asking you first, but only for a message pattern you have confirmed at least three times. Anything else waits for your review. Automatically saved transactions can still be wrong, so check them; you can edit or delete them and turn this feature off at any time.

The app does not send, delete or mark your messages as read, does not request permission to send SMS and does not use a notification-listener service. The companion website cannot read your phone’s SMS inbox. You can paste a message manually without granting SMS permission.

Reading or opening an SMS does not upload your inbox. Raw SMS inbox bodies, messages waiting in the capture queue and the local SMS archive are excluded from the current Android relational sync. Financial details saved as transactions, whether by you or by automatic recording, and classification/format rules the app remembers can be included in backups and cloud sync. These rules may contain sender identifiers, account-matching text or short surrounding text from a message. Notes or documents you deliberately save may also contain message information. You may revoke SMS permissions in Android Settings at any time; manual tracking remains available.

4. Receipts, documents, photos and device features

Receipt text recognition runs on the device or in your browser using OCR software. An image is not sent to an external AI service for OCR. If you save a receipt image, warranty attachment or project document into your records, that saved content can be backed up and synchronized with those records.

The app uses system camera or file-selection interfaces when you request them. Browser camera/file access depends on your browser’s permission and selection controls. On Android, notification permission supports reminders you enable, SMS capture notices and the daily highlights notification. Home-screen widgets may display financial information where other people can see your screen. Device lock or biometric verification, where available, is handled by Android; the app does not receive or store your fingerprint or face template.

5. Mentor and on-device AI (Android)

Mentor gives budgeting and planning guidance worked out on your phone from your own records. It can be wrong and is not professional financial advice. It does not move money or save anything without your action.

Gemini Nano. On phones where Android offers it through AICore, Mentor can use Gemini Nano to word its answers and understand your questions, and to suggest a category for a merchant the app does not recognise. You can turn this off on the Mentor screen. The model receives your question, Mentor’s own answer, a short summary of totals (such as strategy, spending, budget, score and goals) and any notes you gave Mentor, or the merchant name for a category suggestion. This runs on your phone, and the app does not send these prompts or replies to us or to an external AI service. Android and Google manage the model on your phone, including its download.

Google ML Kit. The app uses Google’s ML Kit software to reach Gemini Nano. According to Google’s ML Kit data disclosure, ML Kit sends Google, over HTTPS, the following for diagnostics and usage analytics: device information (such as manufacturer, model, OS version and available machine-learning hardware), the app’s package name and version, user, device or per-installation identifiers, performance metrics, API configuration, input and output sizes, feature version, event types, error codes and configured languages. Google states that it does not transfer this data to third parties. This disclosure does not list your financial records, SMS messages, prompts or replies.

What is stored. Your Mentor chat (the latest 200 messages per profile) is kept in encrypted storage on your phone and is not synced; Forget chat history deletes it. Replies you report are kept on your phone only. Your chosen strategy, the notes you give Mentor and your Mentor preferences are saved with your app settings, so they can be included in backups and cloud sync.

Voice input. If you tap the microphone, the app opens your phone’s speech recognition service, asks it to work offline where possible and receives only the resulting text. The app does not request microphone permission. That service is provided by your phone’s software and is covered by its provider’s terms.

Optional offline model. On phones without Gemini Nano, you can choose to download a small Gemma model from Google (about 550 MB) so Mentor can word its answers on the phone. It downloads only when you tap Download, only on Wi-Fi, from the app’s own public release page on GitHub. Like any download, GitHub receives your IP address and standard request details to deliver the files; the app sends no account, financial or personal information with the request. The model is checked, stored in the app’s private storage and used only on your phone, with the same prompts and limits as Gemini Nano. Remove it at any time on the Mentor screen. Gemma is provided under and subject to the Gemma Terms of Use.

About Mentor counts. To show you how Mentor is working (suggestions taken or put off, suggested categories kept, AI wording that failed its number check, replies you reported, and your record of cycles within budget and goals on track), the app keeps simple counts on your phone. They are not synced or sent anywhere, and Clear these counts deletes them.

Sharing a cycle wrap. If you tap Share as picture, the app draws a picture of your cycle wrap showing categories, percentages and goal names but no amounts, and opens your phone’s share sheet. It goes only to the app or person you pick.

6. Local storage, sign-in and cloud synchronization

Android: app vault records, account sessions and synchronization journals are stored locally using encryption backed by Android Keystore. Original offline records and Google-account profiles are kept separately. Google sign-in enables cloud synchronization with Supabase; signing out does not automatically delete either cloud records or retained local profiles.

Web: the companion app uses browser storage, including IndexedDB and authentication session storage, to keep a local working copy and maintain sign-in. Browser storage does not have the Android vault’s app-managed encryption. Use a trusted device and protect its user account.

Network communication with cloud services uses HTTPS. Cloud records are protected by authentication and account-level access controls. Cloud synchronization is not end-to-end encryption with a key known only to you: authorized service administration and infrastructure providers may access stored data where necessary to operate, support or secure the service. No storage or transmission method is completely secure.

9. Retention and backups

Local records remain until you delete them or remove the relevant app/browser storage. Cloud records remain while your account and records are retained, unless you request deletion. Uninstalling the app, signing out or revoking SMS permission does not delete your cloud account.

Record deletion during synchronization may keep a deletion marker (tombstone) and recovery/journal information so other devices do not recreate deleted records. Therefore deleting a visible record is not the same as requesting complete account/data erasure. Provider backups, security logs and technical recovery copies may persist according to the provider’s configured retention and legal requirements. We will explain any applicable retained data when responding to a verified deletion request rather than promise immediate removal from every backup.

Exported JSON backups and documents remain wherever you save or share them, outside the app’s control. Backup files can contain readable financial information and images; store them securely and delete unwanted copies yourself. Older backups may restore data you previously deleted.

10. Your choices and account/data deletion

You can review and edit your records, export a backup, change notification preferences, turn off automatic SMS recording and Mentor’s on-device AI, delete your Mentor chat and revoke optional device permissions. To request access, correction, deletion of particular cloud records, or deletion of your Financial Tracker / Financial Advisor account and associated cloud data, email im.ahmed.faisal@gmail.com.

For deletion, use the subject Financial Tracker account and data deletion, identify the Google email used to sign in and say whether you want the whole app account deleted or only specified data removed. Send from that email where possible. Do not send your password, verification codes, full bank/card credentials or a financial backup. We may verify ownership before acting to prevent someone else deleting your records. We will confirm the scope and explain any necessary retention exceptions. A request can be made without reinstalling the app or signing into this website.

Account deletion concerns your app authentication profile and associated app cloud records, including financial records, saved documents and learned rules. It does not delete your Google account. Copies already exported/shared, and offline data on devices we cannot access, require separate removal. Stop using sync on other devices while deletion is being processed and remove retained local copies to avoid later re-importing them.

Depending on your location, additional privacy rights may apply, including access, correction, erasure, restriction or objection, portability, and contacting your local data-protection authority. Contact us to exercise applicable rights.
